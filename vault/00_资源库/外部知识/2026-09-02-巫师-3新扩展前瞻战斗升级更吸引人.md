# 《巫师 3》新扩展前瞻：战斗升级更吸引人

## AI 摘要（Horizon 日报）

《巫师 3：狂猎》的新扩展包《过去之歌》在 Gamescom 的闭门预览中展示，但记者 James Archer 认为其表现平平，反而更期待将于 9 月 29 日免费提供给原版玩家的《巫师 3》重制版中的战斗、移动、UI 和角色扮演系统改进。该扩展由 CD Projekt Red 与 Fool&#x27;s Theory 联合开发，而重制版则提前数月发布。预览中展示的内容包括战斗和 UI 的改动，但记者强调这些仅是初步印象。

**「背景」** 《巫师 3：狂猎》是 CD Projekt Red 于 2015 年发行的开放世界角色扮演游戏，以其丰富的叙事和复杂的战斗系统著称。重制版是原版游戏的免费升级，旨在改进画面和游戏机制，而《过去之歌》是新的付费扩展包，延续了杰洛特的故事。

**「影响」** 对于《巫师 3》的现有玩家，重制版的免费升级和战斗改进可能比新扩展更具吸引力，尤其是那些因原版战斗系统而犹豫的玩家。

The Witcher 3: Songs of Past looked alright in its behind-closed-doors preview, though I'm more enticed by the remaster’s combat upgrades
Silver linings
Well, I say behind-closed-doors. It turns out that if I wanted to watch a prerecorded saunter through a chunk of The Witcher III: Wild Hunt’s Songs of the Past expansion, I could have just tuned into the Gamescom livestream, rather than travelling to Germany before parking myself in an almost pitch-black miniature theatre for 45 minutes. Now my by-touch handwritten notes are completely illegible, and I don’t even get to feel smug about attending a super secret preview.
Still, the preview – which like Songs of the Past itself, was a joint operation between CD Projekt Red and co-devs Fool’s Theory – didn’t show off the expansion alone. It also focused repeatedly on the combat, movement, UI and role-playing reworks being introduced in the base game’s Remastered upgrade, which is coming months ahead of Songs of the Past on September 29th. And, usual hands-off disclaimers applying, looks like it could be the more appealing package for lapsed Witchers – especially as it’s free to owners of the original.
Fighting, in particular, appears far more fluid and responsive than Wild Hunt’s current, awkward sword-shuffles. Geralt strings combos together with greater grace and, I suspect, outright faster attacks – I’ve not counted animation frames but he certainly looks like a more nimble swordfighter in the Remaster. Smarter camerawork also gives a better impression of which baddie, when they’re grouped up, is the one being targeted.
It’s not quite reaching Batman Arkham degrees of brawly slickness, and again, it’s impossible to properly judge swordfeel when everything’s being controlled by an unseen developer. But there’s very visible intent here to improve on one of Wild Hunt’s lingering (and, if we’re honest, pretty major) flaws, which is more than most 11-year-old singleplayer games get.
