The No Man's Sky players building utopia at the end of the universe
"People want to help each other."
In the distant reaches of a doomed universe, perpetually 16 minutes from total destruction, something wonderful is happening. Across three galaxies and thousands star systems, a thriving community has formed. This is No Man's Sky's Galactic Hub Project - part scientific endeavour, part social gathering - and in the 10 years since its foundation, it's continued to grow. It's a story of collaboration and kindness, beginning at a time when positivity was low.
10 years ago, the No Man's Sky community was fractured. After a controversial launch in August 2016, there were those unhappy with the exploratory space sim, while others had very much fallen for its procedural charms. Galactic Hub Project founder 7101334 - a now 30-year-old Los Angeles resident who goes by the name Conor out in the real world - was one of those players immediately drawn in by Hello Games' simulated universe; a shared map so vast it was largely unknown even to the studio. But those who loved it felt like a minority at the time.
"Early in No Man's Sky launch, everyone was kind of hating on it for the most part," recalls Conor. "But I was someone who had ordered it and loved it from the start. Maybe it wasn't everything I expected, but I still loved what I got." And as the negativity of launch began to overwhelm No Man's Sky’s main subreddit, those like-minded players enraptured by developer Hello Games' universe-sized enigma found haven elsewhere.
